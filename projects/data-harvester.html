Overview

Data Harvester is an open-source guide that organizes the process of researching publicly available information into a clear, repeatable workflow.

Instead of relying on scattered tools, forum posts, and disconnected techniques, the project brings common OSINT concepts together into one structured reference.

The idea

Information about people, organizations, websites, and online services is often publicly available but spread across many different sources.

I wanted to turn that scattered process into something easier to understand and follow - starting with a known piece of information and progressively researching related public data points.

How it works

The guide follows a step-by-step research methodology rather than depending on a single tool or service.

  • Start with an available piece of public information.
  • Identify relevant publicly accessible sources.
  • Cross-reference information between sources.
  • Document useful findings and relationships.
  • Maintain a clear distinction between verified information and assumptions.

Why I built it

OSINT has legitimate applications across journalism, security research, digital-footprint auditing, and general online investigation.

The goal of Data Harvester is to make the research process more organized and educational while encouraging responsible use of publicly available information.

Responsible use

The project is intentionally limited to passive research of information that is already publicly accessible.

It does not involve unauthorized access, credential theft, bypassing authentication, accessing private accounts, or circumventing platform security controls.

What I learned

The project gave me a better understanding of how seemingly unrelated pieces of public information can be connected through careful research and verification.

More importantly, it reinforced the importance of documenting sources, validating findings, and understanding the boundaries between legitimate research and invasive behavior.
